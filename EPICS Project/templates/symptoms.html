Medical options

Treatment approaches & cures

Modern breast cancer treatment is highly personalised. Understanding your options empowers better decisions with your care team.

01
First-line
Surgery

Surgery remains the most common initial treatment. Options include a lumpectomy (removal of the tumour and a small margin) or a mastectomy (removal of the entire breast). Sentinel lymph node biopsy helps determine if cancer has spread.

Lumpectomy Mastectomy Reconstruction Lymph node biopsy
02
Adjuvant therapy
Chemotherapy

Chemotherapy uses powerful drugs to kill cancer cells or stop them dividing. It may be given before surgery (neoadjuvant, to shrink the tumour) or after surgery to reduce recurrence risk. Protocols are tailored by cancer subtype and stage.

Anthracyclines Taxanes CMF regimen Dose-dense
03
Hormone-positive cancers
Hormone therapy

For oestrogen or progesterone receptor-positive cancers, hormone therapy blocks the body's oestrogen to slow or stop tumour growth. Taken for 5–10 years after primary treatment, it dramatically reduces recurrence rates.

Tamoxifen Aromatase inhibitors Ovarian suppression CDK4/6 inhibitors
04
HER2-positive
Targeted therapy

Targeted therapies attack specific proteins or genes that help cancer grow. Trastuzumab (Herceptin) revolutionised HER2-positive breast cancer treatment. Newer agents like pertuzumab and T-DM1 have further improved outcomes.

Trastuzumab Pertuzumab T-DM1 Lapatinib PARP inhibitors
05
Localised control
Radiation therapy

Radiation therapy uses high-energy rays to destroy remaining cancer cells after surgery. It significantly reduces local recurrence, especially following lumpectomy. Modern techniques like IMRT and proton therapy minimise damage to surrounding tissue.

Whole breast RT Partial breast IMRT Proton therapy
06
Emerging frontier
Immunotherapy

Immunotherapy helps the immune system recognise and destroy cancer cells. Checkpoint inhibitors such as pembrolizumab are now approved for triple-negative breast cancer. CAR-T cell therapy is showing promise in clinical trials for HER2-positive metastatic disease.

Pembrolizumab Atezolizumab CAR-T cells Cancer vaccines
