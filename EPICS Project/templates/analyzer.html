Self-care guide

Early detection saves lives

The earlier breast cancer is found, the greater the chance of successful treatment. Know the steps they could save your life.

1
Monthly breast self-examination
Check your breasts once a month, a few days after your period ends. Look and feel for any changes in shape, skin texture, nipple appearance or any lumps. Report anything unusual to your doctor promptly.
2
Annual clinical breast exam
Women aged 20–40 should have a clinical breast exam by a healthcare provider every 1–3 years. Women over 40 should have one annually. A professional exam detects changes that self-exam may miss.
3
Mammography screening
Annual mammograms are recommended starting at age 40 for average-risk women. Those with a family history or genetic risk may start earlier. Mammography can detect tumours up to 2 years before they become palpable.
4
Ultrasound & MRI for high-risk women
Women with dense breast tissue, a BRCA mutation, or a strong family history may benefit from supplemental screening with ultrasound or breast MRI, which offers higher sensitivity than mammography alone.
5
Know the warning signs
Seek immediate medical advice if you notice: a new lump or thickening, changes in breast size or shape, skin dimpling or redness, nipple discharge or inversion, or persistent breast pain. Early reporting is key.
