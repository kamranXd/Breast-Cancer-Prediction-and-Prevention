Prevention & risk reduction

Up to 40% of breast cancer cases may be preventable through lifestyle changes and early awareness. Here is what the evidence says.

🏃
Stay physically active
Regular moderate exercise at least 150 minutes per week reduces breast cancer risk by up to 20%. Physical activity helps regulate hormones such as oestrogen and insulin that can fuel tumour growth.
Lifestyle
🥦
Eat a plant-rich diet
Diets high in vegetables, fruits, legumes and whole grains, and low in processed foods and red meat, are linked to lower breast cancer incidence. Cruciferous vegetables contain compounds that may help suppress tumour cells.
Nutrition
🚭
Avoid tobacco & limit alcohol
Smoking and alcohol consumption are established risk factors. Even one alcoholic drink a day slightly increases risk. Quitting smoking and reducing alcohol intake can meaningfully reduce your risk over time.
Lifestyle
⚖️
Maintain a healthy weight
Being overweight after menopause significantly raises breast cancer risk. Fat tissue produces extra oestrogen, which can stimulate breast cancer cells. Even modest weight loss can reduce risk noticeably.
Lifestyle
🤱
Breastfeeding reduces risk
Breastfeeding for a cumulative duration of 12 months or more is associated with a modest reduction in breast cancer risk, particularly for hormone-receptor-positive cancers.
Reproductive health
💊
Know your hormone therapy risks
Combined hormone replacement therapy (HRT) taken long-term after menopause is linked to a higher breast cancer risk. Discuss with your doctor whether HRT is right for you and explore alternatives.
Medical
🧬
Understand your genetic risk
BRCA1 and BRCA2 gene mutations significantly increase lifetime risk of breast and ovarian cancer. Genetic counselling and testing can guide preventive strategies including enhanced screening or prophylactic surgery.
Genetics
😴
Prioritise sleep & stress management
Disrupted circadian rhythm (e.g. night shift work) is associated with elevated breast cancer risk. Adequate sleep and stress management support immune function and hormonal balance.
Wellbeing
☢️
Limit radiation exposure
Avoid unnecessary chest X-rays or CT scans, especially in younger women and children. If you have had radiation therapy to the chest before age 30, discuss enhanced screening with your doctor.
